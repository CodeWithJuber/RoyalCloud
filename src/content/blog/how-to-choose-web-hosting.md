Choosing web hosting can feel overwhelming — there are dozens of plan types, confusing acronyms and prices that range from a couple of dollars to hundreds per month. This guide cuts through the noise and helps you pick the right plan based on what your site actually needs.

## Start with three questions

Before comparing providers, answer these:

1. **What kind of site are you running?** A blog, a business brochure site, an online store and a SaaS app all have different needs.
2. **How much traffic do you expect?** Be realistic for the next 6–12 months.
3. **How much do you want to manage yourself?** Some people enjoy server control; most just want it to work.

Your answers point you toward one of four hosting types.

## Shared hosting — best for most websites

With [shared hosting](/shared-hosting), your site lives on a server alongside others, sharing its resources. It's the most affordable option and the easiest to use, with a control panel and 1-click installers.

**Choose it if:** you're launching a blog, portfolio, small business site or a store with light-to-moderate traffic. Modern SSD + LiteSpeed shared hosting is far more capable than the shared hosting of a decade ago.

## Managed WordPress — shared hosting, tuned for WordPress

[Managed WordPress hosting](/managed-wordpress-hosting) is shared hosting optimised specifically for WordPress: automatic updates, daily backups, staging sites and caching configured for you.

**Choose it if:** WordPress is your whole world and you'd rather not think about updates, security or performance tuning.

## VPS — your own slice of a server

A [KVM VPS](/kvm-vps-hosting) gives you guaranteed CPU, RAM and storage with full root access. You're not competing with neighbours for resources, and you can install whatever you like.

**Choose it if:** you've outgrown shared hosting, run custom software, or want predictable performance for a busy site or app.

## Dedicated & managed cloud — maximum power

A [dedicated server](/dedicated-servers) gives you an entire physical machine. [Managed cloud](/managed-digitalocean-cloud-hosting) gives you scalable cloud resources without the sysadmin work.

**Choose it if:** you have high, sustained traffic, strict compliance needs, or resource-hungry workloads.

## Don't forget the fundamentals

Whatever tier you choose, insist on these:

- **SSD/NVMe storage** — never settle for HDD in 2026.
- **Free SSL** — non-negotiable for security and SEO.
- **Daily backups** and easy restores.
- **Real 24/7 support** — test their live chat *before* you buy.
- **A money-back guarantee** so you can try risk-free.

## Our recommendation

Most people should start with **SSD shared or managed WordPress hosting** and upgrade to a VPS when traffic demands it. Good hosting scales with you — so pick a provider that offers the whole ladder, not just one rung.
